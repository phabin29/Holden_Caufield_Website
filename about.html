DRAFT

About Me, I Guess.

Look. If you're reading this, you probably want to know all the usual stuff — where I was born, and what my childhood was like, and what my parents did before they had me, and all that David Copperfield kind of crapbiography nonsense. I'm not going to do that. I'm going to tell you what I want to tell you, and you can take it or leave it. Nobody should have to make an "About Me" page anyway. It's depressing as hell. But the guy running this whole site said I had to. So here we are.

The Basics. Not That They Matter.

Name's Holden V. Caulfield. The V. is for nothing. It's on my birth certificate and I've never used it, not once. I'm from New York. The city, I mean. I was born there, around 1933, which makes me sixteen — though I'll be seventeen pretty soon, not that it'll improve me any. I'm tall. Six foot two and a half, if it's any of your business, which it isn't. I grew six and a half inches in one year. The doctors said it could've been the reason my health got lousy, all that growing. I don't know. I'm inclined to blame the cigarettes.

I've got a streak of gray hair on the right side of my head, right where everybody can see it. I've had it since I was a little kid. I'm not kidding — millions of gray hairs, right there in a clump. It's ridiculous. Sometimes I use it to pretend I'm older than I am, and sometimes other people just pretend for me, which is worse, if you think about it.

I smoke too much. My lungs aren't great. I'm a lousy athlete. I was the manager of the fencing team at Pencey, which isn't even a real thing to be the manager of, and I lost all the foils on the subway. That's about the only thing I'm famous for, if you want the truth.

A Brief, Unhappy History of My Education.

Four schools so far. Four. Pencey Prep, in Agerstown, Pennsylvania, is the latest one to be done with me. I flunked four subjects out of five this term. Before that there was Whooton School, and Elkton Hills, and one other one even further back that isn't worth mentioning. I actually read a lot, in case you were wondering. I finished a whole Thomas Hardy novel on my own, which nobody at Pencey would've believed if I told them. It's not a brains problem. It's a phonies problem.

All four schools were crawling with them — the kind of guys who shake your hand too hard and call everybody "fellas" and act like they know God personally. It makes your skin crawl. It really does. The headmasters, especially. Man, the headmasters. You would not believe the headmasters.

A Kid Named James Castle.

I haven't told anybody about this in a long time. But if we're doing the whole "About Me" business, you might as well know about James Castle, because he's in my head a lot. He was at Elkton Hills. Skinny kid. Wrists like pencils. A bunch of older guys were trying to make him take back something he'd said about another boy, who was conceited, which James happened to be right about, and he wouldn't take it back. So they — well. He went out a window instead of taking it back.

He was wearing my turtleneck sweater when he did it. I'd lent it to him maybe a week before.

I think about that a lot more than I let on. I probably shouldn't have put it in here. But there it is.

My Family. Briefly.

My older brother D.B. used to write real stories — one of them called "The Secret Goldfish" that I still think about sometimes — but he moved out to Hollywood and now he's basically a prostitute. Meaning he writes for the movies. Movies are phony, and anyone who takes that kind of money to ruin what they were good at is a prostitute, in my opinion. He was in the war before that, the whole four years of it, and he came back not quite the same person. He bought a Jaguar. I don't feel like talking about D.B.

My mother is my mother. She's been nervous ever since Allie died. My father is a corporation lawyer. They've got different religions from each other, him and my mother, which I guess makes us kids sort of atheists, or close enough. We don't really talk.

Phoebe is ten. She's the smartest person I know, and I'm not exaggerating. She has red hair and she's skinny and she's the only person in my whole family I can actually tell things to. You'd like her. Everybody who meets her likes her. She writes mystery books about a girl detective named Hazle Weatherfield. She never finishes them, but the pages she does finish, you want to keep.

Allie.

My brother Allie died of leukemia when I was thirteen and he was eleven. That was July 18, 1946. He had red hair. He was the nicest person in my whole family. He was the smartest one too, if you want the truth.

He was a left-handed pitcher, and he wrote poems all over his baseball glove in green ink — a lot of them — so he'd have something to read when he was out in the field and nobody was at bat. I still have the glove. I'm not going to tell you what I do with it.

The night he died I broke every window in our garage. Just with my fist. I'm not really sure why, or I do know and I don't feel like getting into it. My hand still doesn't close all the way when it rains.

I don't want to talk about Allie anymore. I shouldn't have started.

The Hat.

Bought it in New York the morning after I lost the foils. Paid a dollar for it. It's red — very red — with one of those long bills that turns backwards, which is how I wear it. It looks ridiculous. That's the whole point. I wear it when I want to feel like myself, which I'll admit isn't as often as it should be.

I'm wearing it in the photograph up top, if you were wondering. You can't see my face all that well, which is deliberate.

What I Want to Be.

Everybody asks this. Phoebe asked me once, right to my face, and she wasn't going to let me get away with any of my usual baloneylies. So here it is.

I don't want to be a lawyer like my father. Lawyers are fine if what they do is save innocent people, but even then you'd spend the whole time wondering if you were doing it because you wanted to save lives or because you wanted to be the kind of guy who saves lives. Those are two different things, and one of them is phonier than the other. Probably worse.

I don't want to be a businessman. I don't want to be a scientist. I don't want to be a writer either — no offense to whoever's reading this.

What I'd like to be, and this is going to sound stupid, is the only person standing at the edge of a big cliff, where a bunch of little kids are playing in a field of rye, and my whole job is to catch them before they go over. That's it. That's the whole thing. I know how it sounds. I can't help it. It's the only thing I've ever wanted to do that felt like it'd actually be worth doing.

Things That Kill Me.

(Meaning I love them. I know it's confusing. It's just how I talk.)

Phoebe dancing in her pajamas. The ducks at the lagoon, wherever they're getting to. Allie's glove. Museums where nothing has moved in a hundred years. Jane Gallagher keeping her kings in the back row when we played checkers. That little kid walking along the curb on Broadway, singing to himself, while his parents didn't even notice. The carousel, when Phoebe rode it in the rain.

Things That Don't.

Phonies. Guys who say "grand." Guys who say "old boy." Movies, mostly. People who clap in between pieces at the piano. Guys who take girls out just to say they took them out. Funerals. Christmas, usually. Most of what adults tell you about anything. Especially what they tell you about growing up.

And myself. Most of the time. Especially myself, if you want the truth.

Where I Am Right Now.

If you were wondering, which you probably weren't, I'm writing this from a rest place out in California. That's all I want to say about it. I came out here a little while after everything I keep telling people about. My D.B. drives out from Hollywood every weekend in the Jaguar, so at least someone visits. The guys here keep asking if I'm going to apply myself when I go back to school next September. I don't know. How do you know what you're going to do until you do it? It's a stupid question.

Alright. That's Enough.

I said I wasn't going to tell you a whole bunch of stuff, and I've told you a whole bunch of stuff. I'm lousy at keeping promises. Ask anybody who knows me.

If you read all this, I don't really know what to say. Thanks, I guess. I'd tell you not to expect too much from me, but you probably already figured that out.

I do mean the "thanks" though. Don't tell anybody.

— December, somewhere in California.
